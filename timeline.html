Interactive Timeline: 1815 to the Modern Era

Trace the incredible milestones of Ada Lovelace's life, from her turbulent childhood to her lasting legacy in the modern computing era.

Milestones in Ada Lovelace's Life
Year Event

1815

 December 10: Augusta Ada Byron is born in London to Lord Byron and Anne Isabella Milbanke.

1816

 Her parents legally separate; Lord Byron leaves England permanently, leaving Ada to be raised solely by her mother.

1828

 At age 12, Ada designs a mechanical flying horse powered by steam and writes her illustrated manuscript Flyology.

1829-1832

 Survives a severe bout of measles that leaves her temporarily paralyzed; she uses her recovery time to master advanced geometry and algebra.

1833

 June: Meets Charles Babbage in London and views a working model of his Difference Engine, beginning a 19-year collaboration.

1835

 July 8: Marries William King, 8th Baron King.

1838

 Her husband is elevated to Earl of Lovelace, and Ada officially becomes the Countess of Lovelace.

1840

 Begins correspondence-based advanced calculus and logic lessons with renowned mathematician Augustus De Morgan.

1842

 Italian mathematician Luigi Menabrea publishes a French paper describing Babbage’s proposed Analytical Engine after hearing Babbage lecture in Turin.

1843

 September: Ada publishes her English translation of Menabrea’s paper in Taylor's Scientific Memoirs, appending seven extensive "Notes" (A through G) that triple the length of the original article.

1852

 November 27: Passes away in London from uterine cancer at age 36.

1953

 Over a century after her death, her 1843 Notes are republished in B.V. Bowden's Faster Than Thought, sparking modern recognition of her role in computing history.

1979

 The United States Department of Defense names its new standardized high-level programming language "Ada" in her honor.

2009

 The first annual Ada Lovelace Day is launched to celebrate the achievements of women in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM).
